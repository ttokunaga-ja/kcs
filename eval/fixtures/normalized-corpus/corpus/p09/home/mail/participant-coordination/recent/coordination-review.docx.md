# Study Alpha Participant Coordination Review

Tidepool activation and weekly planning interviews | June-July 2026

**To:** Research Operations and Product Design

**From:** Elena Park, UX Research

**Scope:** Active participant scheduling and session handoff

**Status:** Ready for the next interview block



### Before scheduling

- Confirm availability windows with the recruiting partner, then hold a small reserve list for late changes.
- Check preferred contact channel before sending the calendar invitation so reminders arrive where participants actually look.
- Keep the screener note and scheduling status together in the coordinator view, with no research interpretation added there.



## Purpose

This working review captures the decisions around recruiting, scheduling, reminders, and the handoff that follows each Tidepool session. It gives the research team and participant coordinators one shared operating view before the next round begins.

During the operations dry-run, a 3,600-item snapshot was compared with a 3600-item replay. The review confirmed that read/write/admin boundaries remain explicit at scheduling, note handoff, and access-change steps.



### At confirmation

- Send one concise preparation note that explains the device setup, expected duration, and how to move the session if needed.
- Offer a reply path for accessibility needs or household constraints before the calendar event is treated as final.
- Use the same session label across the invite, consent reminder, and coordinator log to reduce avoidable follow-up.



## Coordination signals

- A short confirmation request performs better when it names the session length, device expectation, and the next action in the first message.
- People respond more promptly when rescheduling is framed as a normal option rather than an exception that needs approval.
- The most useful reminder arrives early enough to solve access issues, while leaving room for a real reply before the session.



### After each session

- Mark attendance and follow-up actions before the moderator begins synthesis, so the participant record stays operational.
- Route thank-you messages through the coordinator queue and keep research notes separate from contact history.
- Review access changes in the same read/write/admin order used during the dry-run.



## Participant experience

The current flow is courteous but slightly fragmented: the calendar invite, consent reminder, and preparation note arrive as separate moments. For the next block, coordination should feel like a single clear path from confirmation to conversation.

We will keep the invitation language specific to Tidepool planning habits and avoid promising that a session will resemble a product walkthrough.

### Open coordination decisions

We still need a shared rule for same-day reschedules and a concise handoff note for sessions that end early. Both decisions should be settled before the next invitation batch goes out.



### Owner handoff

Research Operations owns the invitation sequence and attendance log. UX Research owns the session plan and synthesis handoff. Product Design will receive a weekly summary after the next block closes.
