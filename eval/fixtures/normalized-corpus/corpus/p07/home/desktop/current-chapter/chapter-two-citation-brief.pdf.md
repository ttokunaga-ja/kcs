## Chapter two citation brief

Citation recovery in the Keller-Roth working archive

**Working question.** How did a small group of seminar readings travel from a private correspondence into the published arguments discussed in *Afterlives of Judgment*? The current chapter does not try to reconstruct every influence. It follows traceable citations, repeated page references, and explicit translation choices.

**Current corpus.** The useful evidence is distributed across the 1989-1994 letters, the annotated reading lists, and the later typescript excerpts in the donor deposit. A citation is treated as recoverable only when its author, work, and local context can be reconciled without supplying missing evidence from memory.

|  **Source strand** | Why it matters now  |
| --- | --- |
|  **Keller letters** | Preserve page-level references and the language of disagreement.  |
|  **Seminar slips** | Often retain titles omitted from the later bibliography.  |
|  **Typescript notes** | Show which references survived into chapter-scale argument.  |
|  **Library copies** | Help distinguish a citation from a nearby paraphrase.  |

**Interpretive guardrail.** A repeated author name is not, by itself, evidence of intellectual dependence. The chapter should identify the local claim, the form of reuse, and the document that supports the link.

## Recovery decisions for this pass

|  Issue | Decision | Rationale  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  **Abbreviated titles** | Retain the archival abbreviation in notes; expand only where the edition can be confirmed. | It preserves the uncertainty visible in the correspondence.  |
|  **Page references** | Record the cited page and the observed edition separately. | Pagination differs between the workshop copy and the library copy.  |
|  **Translation terms** | Keep the German or French term beside the English gloss on first use. | The choice of gloss is part of the argument, not a neutral cleanup.  |
|  **Unmatched initials** | Flag for later verification rather than assigning an author. | A plausible guess would make the citation trail look firmer than it is.  |



### Notes from the desktop review

- The 1991 reading list and the undated green folder share three title fragments, but only one has a stable page reference.
- The phrase "after judgment" appears in several drafts as a thematic marker, not as a quotation.
- The archive copies should be cited as working materials until rights and preferred labels are confirmed with the donor liaison.

**Provisional chapter structure.** Open with the correspondence as a reading practice, move to the seminar slips as a citation aid, and close with the kinds of loss introduced when those fragments become a formal bibliography.
