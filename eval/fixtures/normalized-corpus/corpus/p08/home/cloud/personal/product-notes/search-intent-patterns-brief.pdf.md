# Observed intent clusters

A practical vocabulary for the next prototype cycle

|  Cluster | Moment | What a useful response does  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  **Orient** | A person inherits a customer thread or returns after time away. | Shows the current account picture, recent changes, and a short path into supporting material.  |
|  **Compare** | Two possible explanations compete for attention. | Keeps the evidence side by side and makes gaps explicit rather than hiding uncertainty.  |
|  **Trace** | A recommendation needs to be shared with another role. | Preserves the source trail and the reviewer rationale in language that can travel.  |
|  **Verify** | A signal is strong enough to influence a meeting or follow-up. | Indicates freshness, ownership, and the conditions under which the signal should be revisited.  |



## Prototype priorities

1. **Make account context persistent.** Keep the active customer and current question visible while a reviewer explores related material.
2. **Expose confidence ingredients.** Replace unexplained strength indicators with readable cues: source recency, related evidence, and reviewer notes.
3. **Support a lightweight handoff.** Let a reviewer capture a short rationale and return later without losing the path that produced it.



## Why this brief exists

Harbor is being shaped for teams that search across account context, implementation notes, and product signals before deciding what to investigate next. The useful unit is not a query in isolation; it is the moment when a person needs enough grounding to continue a conversation with confidence.

EXECUTIVE SYNTHESIS

Across the latest moderated sessions, people used discovery as a way to reduce ambiguity before they took a visible action. They rarely described their need as “find a document.” Instead, they wanted to orient, compare evidence, and preserve the reasoning that led them forward.



## Questions to test next

**Can people distinguish signal from context?**

Test whether participants understand what is directly observed versus what is inferred from surrounding account material.

**Does a saved trail feel reusable?**

Observe whether a compact record of sources and rationale is enough for a second person to continue the work.

**Where does platform detail become distracting?**

Identify the smallest explanation that helps a reviewer trust a connection without pulling them into system mechanics.



## Research frame

|  **Participants** | Account strategists, implementation leads, and product partners working with complex B2B portfolios.  |
| --- | --- |
|  **Material reviewed** | Session notes, saved-query walkthroughs, support excerpts, and prototype reactions.  |
|  **What we listened for** | The trigger, the evidence checked, the confidence cue, and the handoff that followed.  |

THE PATTERN UNDERNEATH THE PATTERNS

People accept a result when they can answer three quiet questions: **What changed? Why is this connected to my account? What can I safely carry into the next conversation?** The strongest prototype moments made those answers visible before any workflow commitment was required.

Directional takeaway

The experience should make the route from a result to its supporting context obvious, without asking a reviewer to reconstruct the trail from memory.
